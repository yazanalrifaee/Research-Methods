# Lecture 01 — Key Concepts

| Term | Lecture-based meaning |
|---|---|
| Research | Systematic approach to creating new knowledge. |
| Theory | Combination of statements and derivation rules used to predict outcomes. |
| Theory formation | Developing understanding that enables predictions. |
| Research question | The precisely defined problem driving the research. |
| Study design | The approach taken to find an answer. |
| Inquiry Cycle | Define problem, design, execute, analyze, revise theory if needed, publish. |
| Empirical research | Gathers information from real observation, possibly in a controlled environment. |
| Non-empirical research | Includes theoretical work, particularly mathematical work. |
| Primary research | Evaluates a question directly. |
| Secondary research | Combines information from primary research for a more complete picture. |
| Tertiary research | Combines secondary research. |
| Replicable | Other researchers can repeat the research. |
| Refutable | Research is open to challenge; flaws can in principle be found. |
| Peer review | Evaluation of research by knowledgeable peers before publication. |
| Explanatory research goal | Understand or explain phenomena. |
| Engineering research goal | Create methods or technical artefacts to achieve desired outcomes. |

## Important distinctions
- **Theory vs study design:** Theory is current understanding; study design is the planned approach to investigate a question.
- **Empirical vs non-empirical:** Observation of reality vs theoretical work; both have a place.
- **Primary vs secondary vs tertiary:** Direct investigation vs synthesis of primary work vs synthesis of secondary work.
- **Explanation vs construction:** Understanding why/how something happens vs creating something using knowledge.
- **Peer-reviewed vs certainly correct:** Peer review is a quality mechanism, not a guarantee.
