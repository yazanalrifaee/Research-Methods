# Lecture 01 — Flashcards

1. **Q:** What is research?  
   **A:** A systematic approach to creating new knowledge.

2. **Q:** Why use research methods?  
   **A:** To avoid false conclusions, make work repeatable, and make findings believable.

3. **Q:** What is the typical goal of research?  
   **A:** Theory formation: understanding that enables predictions.

4. **Q:** How do theory and experiment interact?  
   **A:** Theory predicts; observations/experiments provide outcomes; outcomes inform theory.

5. **Q:** List the Inquiry Cycle.  
   **A:** Define problem/RQ → design study → execute → analyze → revise theory if needed → publish.

6. **Q:** What is a theory?  
   **A:** Statements plus derivation rules that allow predictions.

7. **Q:** What are the two purposes of theory?  
   **A:** Explanation and construction.

8. **Q:** What is mathematics' special role in the lecture?  
   **A:** It reasons within a theory and derives consequences using rules.

9. **Q:** Is CS research like natural science or engineering?  
   **A:** It depends on the goal; it can be explanatory or engineering-oriented.

10. **Q:** What makes a research question valid?  
    **A:** It supports prediction and can be answered in principle.

11. **Q:** Why is “What is the best development method?” weak?  
    **A:** It is unbounded and cannot be operationalized over all possible methods.

12. **Q:** What does study design answer?  
    **A:** How to generate knowledge / approach the research question.

13. **Q:** What factors determine study design?  
    **A:** Research question, context/feasibility, and required result quality.

14. **Q:** What are three characteristics of good research?  
    **A:** Well-documented, replicable, refutable.

15. **Q:** What is a workshop publication?  
    **A:** Often preliminary work and a venue for discussion.

16. **Q:** What is a conference publication?  
    **A:** Typically validated work with limited publication length.

17. **Q:** What is a journal publication?  
    **A:** Larger, more detailed work.

18. **Q:** Does peer review guarantee correctness?  
    **A:** No; reviewers can miss flaws and the process can have social limitations.

19. **Q:** Define primary research.  
    **A:** Directly investigates/evaluates a question.

20. **Q:** Define secondary research.  
    **A:** Synthesizes primary research to create a broader picture.

21. **Q:** Define tertiary research.  
    **A:** Synthesizes secondary research.

22. **Q:** Empirical vs non-empirical?  
    **A:** Empirical observes reality; non-empirical includes theoretical work.

23. **Q:** What are method-selection dimensions mentioned?  
    **A:** Empirical/formal and technology/people oriented.

24. **Q:** What research purposes can influence method choice?  
    **A:** Explore concepts/hypotheses, validate hypotheses, map the research frontier.

25. **Q:** What is the main limitation of scientific knowledge?  
    **A:** It is not guaranteed infallible; results may be flawed, so research remains open to scrutiny.
