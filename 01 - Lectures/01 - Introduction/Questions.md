# Lecture 01 — Questions for Understanding

1. **Why are research methods necessary?** They help avoid false conclusions, support repeatability, and make findings more believable.
2. **How does theory interact with experiment?** Theory predicts outcomes; experiments/observations produce real outcomes that may support or prompt revision of theory.
3. **What are the stages of the Inquiry Cycle?** Precisely define the problem, design the study, execute it, analyze results, revise theory if needed, and publish results.
4. **What makes a research question valid according to the lecture?** It supports prediction and can at least in principle be answered.
5. **Why is “What is the best development method?” problematic?** Scope is unbounded and “best” is not operationally defined.
6. **What determines study design?** Research question, context factors, and required result qualities, including threats to validity.
7. **What are the three characteristics of good research?** Well-documented, replicable, and refutable.
8. **How do workshops, conferences, and journals differ?** Workshops commonly discuss preliminary work; conferences publish validated work with limited length; journals publish larger, more detailed work.
9. **Does peer review guarantee correctness?** No. Reviewers may miss issues, influence the paper, and closed communities may weaken the process.
10. **Distinguish primary, secondary, and tertiary research.** Primary directly investigates; secondary synthesizes primary research; tertiary synthesizes secondary research.
11. **Is empirical research always better?** No; the lecture says empirical and non-empirical research both have a place.
12. **What guides method selection?** Research question, replication scale, and whether the goal is concept/hypothesis development, hypothesis validation, or identifying the research frontier.
13. **Are scientific results guaranteed correct?** No; errors in observation, reasoning, or empirical setup remain possible.
