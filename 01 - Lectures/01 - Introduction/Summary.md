# Lecture 01 — Introduction: Summary

## Main message
Good research uses a systematic, appropriate, transparent method to create new knowledge that others can assess and build on.

## Essential points
1. **Why methods matter:** Without them, conclusions may be false, work may not be repeatable, and findings may not be trusted.
2. **Research:** A systematic approach to creating new knowledge, typically contributing to theory formation and prediction.
3. **Theory and experiment:** Theory predicts; experiment/observation provides a real outcome; outcomes inform theory.
4. **Inquiry Cycle:** Precisely define problem → design study → execute study → analyze results → revise theory if needed → publish results.
5. **Theory:** Statements plus derivation rules that support prediction; it can explain phenomena or support engineering construction.
6. **CS/IS research:** Can be explanatory (understanding real-world phenomena) or engineering-oriented (creating methods and artefacts), depending on the goal.
7. **Valid research question:** Supports prediction and is answerable in principle.
8. **Study design:** The approach for generating knowledge; selected according to the question, context, and required result quality.
9. **Good research:** Well-documented, replicable, and refutable.
10. **Publication:** Workshops generally discuss preliminary work; conferences present validated, shorter work; journals provide larger, detailed work. Peer review helps quality but is not infallible.
11. **Research layers:** Primary directly investigates; secondary synthesizes primary; tertiary synthesizes secondary.
12. **Empirical vs non-empirical:** Empirical relies on observation of reality; non-empirical includes theoretical work. Both are legitimate.
13. **Method selection:** Consider the question (people, technical artefacts, or theory), replication opportunities, and whether the aim is exploration, validation, or mapping the research frontier.
14. **Scientific certainty:** Research results are not guaranteed correct; systematic research is still the strongest approach described in the lecture for identifying solid knowledge.

## Course roadmap from the lecture
- Fundamental: metrics, statistics, threats to validity.
- Primary: (human) experiments, case studies, technical experiments, formal methods.
- Secondary: systematic literature review, mapping studies.
- Auxiliary: grounded theory.
