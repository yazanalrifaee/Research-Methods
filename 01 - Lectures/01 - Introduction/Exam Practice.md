## Application questions
### A. Improve a research question
Weak: “What is the best testing method?”

Possible improvement: “Among methods A, B, and C, which identifies the highest number of defects in a defined software system under a specified time budget?”

Explain why the revised question is more answerable: it bounds the methods, defines an outcome, and establishes context.

### B. Classify research
- Observe how developers work in a team → explanatory, empirical, people-oriented.
- Build a new automated testing tool → engineering/construction-oriented; may include technical experiments.
- Prove an algorithm's property → formal/theoretical, non-empirical.
- Synthesize results from published studies → secondary research.

### C. Choose a method
For a question about user behavior, consider human-oriented methods. For technical performance of an artefact, consider technical experiments. For consequences derived from a theory, formal reasoning may be suitable. Explain the choice in terms of RQ, feasibility, replication, and desired validity.

## Mini mock exam
**1.** Research results are believable simply because they are published. True or false? Explain.  
**2.** What is the difference between a research question and a study design?  
**3.** Can empirical and non-empirical approaches both be legitimate?  
**4.** Why does replication matter?  
**5.** What is the purpose of publishing research?  
**6.** Why can unexpected experimental outcomes be valuable?  
**7.** Explain the difference between explanation and construction in CS research.

## Answer key (brief)
1. False—publication and peer review help assess quality but do not guarantee correctness.
2. RQ specifies what is asked; study design specifies how knowledge/answer will be generated.
3. Yes; each serves different research needs.
4. It lets others check and build upon findings.
5. Communicate methods/results so others can assess, reproduce, and build on them.
6. They can reveal limitations or lead to improved theories.
7. Explanation studies phenomena; construction develops methods/artefacts to achieve desired outcomes.
