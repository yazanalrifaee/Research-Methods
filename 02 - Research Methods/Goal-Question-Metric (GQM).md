# Goal-Question-Metric (GQM)

## Purpose
A systematic framework to derive relevant metrics from goals, ensuring data collection supports a research or improvement objective.

## Structure
Goal → Question → Metric.

### Goal
Defines what is being analyzed, why, the quality focus, perspective, and context.

### Questions
Operationalize the goal: what must be understood or answered to evaluate it?

### Metrics
Measurements that provide evidence to answer the questions. A metric can support several questions.

## Goal template
Analyze **[object]** for the purpose of **[purpose]**, with respect to **[quality focus]**, from the point of view of **[viewpoint]**, in the context of **[environment]**.

## Four phases
1. Planning
2. Definition (goal, questions, metrics)
3. Data collection
4. Interpretation

## Direction
- Top-down derivation: goal → questions → metrics.
- Bottom-up interpretation: measurements → answers → goal assessment.

## Example: code reviews
Goal: understand structured reviews' effectiveness in fault detection and learning in a specific development team/project.

Questions may include:
- What faults are detected?
- What review effort is spent?
- What document characteristics affect outcomes?
- Are reviews useful for training?

Metrics may include defect counts/types/severity, effort, document size/complexity, reviewer, date, and review context.

## Common mistakes
- Collecting many metrics without a goal.
- Using context-independent metric definitions.
- Deriving metrics that do not answer a question.
- Failing to define measurement procedures.
- Assuming a metric's scale allows operations that are not justified.
